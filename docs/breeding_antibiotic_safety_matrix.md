# VetDict Breeding Treatment Protocols: Antibiotic Safety Matrix

## Overview
Antibiotic selection for reproductive diseases (mastitis, metritis, dystocia) varies significantly by species due to differences in gut microbiota, metabolic capacity, and GI dysbiosis risk. This document provides species-specific guidance for safe antibiotic use during breeding management.

---

## MASTITIS: First-Line Antibiotic Selection by Species

### Safe Antibiotics Matrix

| **Species** | **First-Line** | **Alternatives** | **CONTRAINDICATED** | **Notes** |
|------------|-----------------|------------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Dog** 🐕 | Cephalexin 25-30 mg/kg PO q6-8h × 14-21d | Amoxicillin-clavulanate 22 mg/kg q8-12h | — | Excellent Staph coverage; safe for nursing puppies; minimal milk transfer |
| **Cat** 🐱 | Cephalexin 25-30 mg/kg PO q6-8h × 14-21d | Amoxicillin-clavulanate 12-25 mg/kg q8-12h | Clindamycin | Minimal milk excretion; safe in nursing queens |
| **Rabbit** 🐰 | Enrofloxacin 5-10 mg/kg SC/PO q12h × 10-14d | Chloramphenicol 50 mg/kg q8h | ❌ **Penicillins, Amoxicillin, Ampicillin, Cephalosporins, Macrolides, Clindamycin** | **FATAL GI dysbiosis risk**; absolute β-lactam contraindication |
| **Ferret** 🦨 | Enrofloxacin 5-10 mg/kg SC q12h × 10-14d | Cephalexin 15-25 mg/kg PO q6-8h × 14d, Amoxicillin-clavulanate 12.5 mg/kg q12h | — | β-lactams relatively safe (unlike rodents); Cephalexin preferred over Amoxicillin-clavulanate |
| **Guinea Pig** 🐹 | Enrofloxacin 5-10 mg/kg PO q12h × 10-14d | TMS 30 mg/kg PO q12h | ❌ **Penicillins, Amoxicillin, Cephalosporins** | Better fluoroquinolone tolerance; β-lactam dysbiosis risk |
| **Chinchilla** 🐭 | Enrofloxacin 5-10 mg/kg PO q12h × 10-14d | TMS 30 mg/kg PO q12h | ❌ **All β-lactams (Penicillins, Cephalosporins)** | **FATAL GI dysbiosis**; small body size accelerates systemic toxicity |
| **Degu** | Enrofloxacin 5-10 mg/kg PO q12h × 10-14d | TMS 30 mg/kg PO q12h | ❌ **β-lactams, Macrolides, Linezolid** | High dysbiosis risk; Linezolid causes myelosuppression |
| **Hedgehog** 🦔 | Enrofloxacin 5-10 mg/kg PO q12h × 10-14d | TMS 30 mg/kg PO q12h | ❌ **β-lactams, Macrolides, Linezolid** | GI flora sensitivity; SC injections preferred |

## Key Clinical Pearls

### 🚨 **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATIONS by Species**

**Rabbits, Guinea Pigs, Chinchillas, Degus, Hedgehogs:**
- ❌ **Penicillins** → Cecal/hindgut dysbiosis (FATAL)
- ❌ **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** → GI dysbiosis (FATAL)
- ❌ **Cephalosporins** → Dysbiosis (FATAL in rodents, chinchillas, hedgehogs)
- ❌ **Macrolides** (clindamycin, erythromycin) → GI upset/dysbiosis

**Why Fatal?** These species lack Clostridium difficile suppression; antibiotic-induced dysbiosis causes irreversible enterotoxemia (typically within 5-14 days).

### ✅ **Species-Specific Advantages**

| Species | First-Line Advantage | Why Different from Dogs/Cats |
|---------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| **Dog/Cat** | Cephalosporin-safe | Robust GI barrier; C. difficile suppression |
| **Rabbit** | Fluoroquinolone-specific | Cecal microbiota extremely sensitive to β-lactams |
| **Ferret** | Limited β-lactam tolerance | Intermediate GI robustness; FQ preferred |
| **Guinea Pig, Chinchilla, Degu, Hedgehog** | FQ/TMS-only | Hindgut fermentation; β-lactam universally contraindicated |

## Mastitis Treatment Protocol: Comparative Timelines

### **Dog & Cat (β-lactam safe)**
```
Duration: 14-21 days
First-line: Cephalexin (excellent penetration, safe nursing)
Alternative: Amoxicillin-clavulanate
Severity escalation: Enrofloxacin if sepsis signs (fever >40°C)
Puppy/Kitten safety: Minimal drug milk transfer; nursing continues
```

### **Rabbit (FQ-only)**
```
Duration: 10-14 days
MUST USE: Enrofloxacin or Chloramphenicol
NEVER: Any β-lactam (FATAL dysbiosis)
Backup: Chloramphenicol 50 mg/kg q8h (if FQ unavailable)
Doe safety: Monitor for GI stasis; supplement hay/veggies
```

### **Ferret (β-lactam conditional)**
```
Duration: 10-14 days
First-line: Enrofloxacin (preferred)
Alternative 1: Cephalexin (if FQ intolerant)
Alternative 2: Amoxicillin-clavulanate (conditional)
Kit care: Supplement feeding <5 days old; nursing >5 days safe
Abscess protocol: +/- Surgical drainage + local irrigation
```

### **Rodents & Hedgehogs (FQ/TMS only)**
```
Duration: 10-14 days
First-line: Enrofloxacin (rapid systemic effect)
Alternative: TMS (if FQ resistance suspected)
CRITICAL: No β-lactams under any circumstances
Dysbiosis risk: Monitor fecal output; syringe-feed supportively if reduced appetite
```

## Decision Algorithm: Choosing Antibiotic by Species

```
START: Mastitis diagnosed
│
├─ IS DOG or CAT?
│  └─→ YES: Use CEPHALEXIN (25-30 mg/kg PO q6-8h × 14-21d)
│           [First-line; excellent safety; safe nursing]
│
├─ IS RABBIT?
│  └─→ YES: Use ENROFLOXACIN (5-10 mg/kg q12h × 10-14d)
│           [Backup: Chloramphenicol if FQ unavailable]
│           ❌ NEVER β-lactams (FATAL dysbiosis)
│
├─ IS FERRET?
│  └─→ YES: Use ENROFLOXACIN (5-10 mg/kg SC q12h × 10-14d)
│           [If FQ intolerant: Cephalexin (preferred over Amoxicillin)]
│
├─ IS GUINEA PIG, CHINCHILLA, DEGU, or HEDGEHOG?
│  └─→ YES: Use ENROFLOXACIN (5-10 mg/kg PO q12h × 10-14d)
│           [Backup: TMS 30 mg/kg q12h]
│           ❌ NEVER β-lactams (FATAL dysbiosis)
│
END: Monitor fever curve; culture-guided adjustment at day 2-3
```

## Post-Treatment Monitoring

### **All Species: Mandatory Follow-up**
- **Day 1-3**: Temperature, appetite, milk character (clear → purulent = possible abscess)
- **Day 3-5**: Culture results available; adjust antibiotic if needed
- **Day 7-10**: Clinical resolution (fever <38.5°C, normal appetite, reduced mammary swelling)
- **Day 14**: Post-treatment assessment; consider spaying if recurrence risk high

### **Species-Specific Concerns**
| Species | Post-Treatment Risk | Monitoring Focus |
|---------|---------------------|------------------|
| Dog/Cat | Gangrenous mastitis (5% if delayed treatment) | Mammary tissue necrosis; possible mastectomy |
| Rabbit | GI stasis (if any dysbiosis occurred) | Fecal output; cecotrophy consumption |
| Ferret | Kit hypoglycemia if syringe-feeding | Blood glucose if lethargic kits |
| Rodents | Secondary dysbiosis from prolonged FQ | Diarrhea; consider probiotics if GI signs |
| Hedgehog | Wound complications if abscessed | Drain site infection; daily flushing |
